--- analysis ---

The scaling check is the clearest signal, and it flips the baseline's
signature completely. Before: throughput was flat-to-declining as
concurrency rose (3547 -> 3143 -> 2485 ops/sec, 1 to 32 clients) — every
writer queued behind one lock that could only do one fsync at a time, so
more concurrency bought more waiting, not more throughput. After: 8
clients hit 10,773 ops/sec (3.4x the before-number at the same
concurrency) and 32 clients hit 9,082 ops/sec (3.65x) — group commit
lets those same 8 or 32 concurrent writers share a handful of real
fsync calls instead of paying for one each, so throughput now actually
scales with concurrency instead of being capped by fsync rate alone.
Every write-heavy tail latency figure (p50/p95/p99) also dropped
substantially at every concurrency level once there was more than one
writer.

The one honest regression: the fully uncontended case (1 client, ratio
0) got *worse* — p50 233us -> 364us. This is real, not noise: even with
nobody to batch with, the leader still pays one short "quiet tick" (50us,
see kGroupCommitQuietTick) waiting to see if anyone else shows up before
it commits to fsyncing alone, plus the added mutex/condition-variable
coordination that group commit needs regardless of whether it ends up
batching anything. That's the standard group-commit trade-off — a small,
bounded latency tax on the guaranteed-solo case in exchange for a large
throughput and tail-latency win the moment there's real concurrent
write load, which is the case that matters most for a server meant to
serve concurrent clients. At very high concurrency (64 clients)
throughput starts giving some of that win back (6177.5 ops/sec, still
2.5x the old flat baseline) as tail latency grows — 64 concurrent
writers is enough to occasionally miss the record cap and delay cap
timing sweet spot, but it's still a clear net win over the pre-group-
commit baseline at every concurrency level measured.

All 114+ existing tests, including crash_recovery_test and every Raft
failure/linearizability test, pass unchanged after this change — see
docs/performance-notes.md for the correctness note on preserving WAL-
order-equals-apply-order across concurrent writers, which group commit
required adding explicitly (previously implicit from one big lock).
