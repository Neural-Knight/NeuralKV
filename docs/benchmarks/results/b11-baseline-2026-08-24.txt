--- analysis: two bottlenecks confirmed by measurement, not guesswork ---

1. DurableStorage's single write_mutex_ fully serializes WAL
   append+fsync+apply, and fsync dominates. The scaling check above is
   the direct evidence: pure-SET throughput does not increase with
   client count — it's flat-to-declining (3547 -> 3143 -> 2485 ops/sec
   from 1 to 32 clients), while p50 latency jumps 8x the moment there's
   more than one concurrent writer (233us -> 1869us) and then stays
   flat. That's the signature of N clients queuing behind one lock that
   can only do one fsync at a time: more concurrency doesn't buy more
   throughput, it just buys more waiting. The write-heavy single-node
   run (80% SET) reflects this directly: p50 of 1848us is almost
   entirely fsync-queue wait, not actual work.

2. RaftNode::ConfirmLeadershipQuorum() sends a full empty-AppendEntries
   round trip to every peer on every single GET, even with zero write
   load. The read-only (ratio 100) comparison isolates this cleanly:
   single-node GET does 198,751 ops/sec at p50=27us; the exact same
   pure-GET workload against a Raft leader does 12,102 ops/sec at
   p50=248us — a 16.4x throughput drop and 9.2x p50 increase for a
   workload that touches zero disk I/O and involves no replication of
   anything. p95/p99 on the Raft leader (2657us/4158us) are worse than
   even the *write-heavy* single-node run's tail, meaning an occasional
   slow quorum round can cost more than an fsync does. This is pure
   avoidable network round-trip cost: the leader already knows it was
   leader a moment ago and nothing requires re-proving that on every
   single read.

Both are addressed in this pass: group commit (B) for #1, quorum-check
amortization (C) for #2. See docs/performance-notes.md for the full
writeup and docs/benchmarks/results/b11-group-commit-2026-08-24.txt /
b11-read-confirm-2026-08-24.txt for before/after numbers.

--- methodology note ---
No sampling profiler (perf, etc.) is available in this container without
elevated privileges Docker doesn't grant by default here, so bottleneck
identification is via targeted comparative benchmarking instead: hold
everything constant except the one variable under suspicion (client
count for the mutex-serialization check; single-node vs. Raft leader for
the quorum-round-trip check) and measure the delta directly. Both deltas
above are large enough (multi-x, not noise) that they don't need finer
instrumentation to be conclusive.
