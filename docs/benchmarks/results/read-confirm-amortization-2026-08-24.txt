Read-confirm amortization (ConfirmLeadershipQuorum), before vs.
after. Docker/Linux, 3-node Raft cluster, --io threadpool --workers 8,
--clients 8 throughout, writing against the elected leader.

"Before" here means: group commit already applied to both runs,
read-confirm amortization specifically disabled for the "before"
run only (the `if (HasRecentMajorityContactLocked()) return true;`
short-circuit in RaftNode::ConfirmLeadershipQuorum forced off) — an
apples-to-apples isolation of just this one change, not a comparison
against an earlier baseline's numbers. optimization-baseline-2026-08-24.txt's
raft-read100 number (12,102 ops/sec) is the true baseline and
lines up with this file's "before" read100 number (12,365.9 ops/sec)
within normal run-to-run variance, confirming group commit alone
doesn't move the read path at all — expected, since it only touches
WalWriter.

command (build): cmake -B build-linux -G Ninja -DCMAKE_BUILD_TYPE=Release
                 && cmake --build build-linux
date: 2026-08-24

--- read-only (ratio 100) — the clean, unconfounded signal ---

before (amortization disabled):
command: nkv-bench --bench --host 127.0.0.1 --port <leader> --duration 15 --clients 8 --ratio 100 --label before-read-confirm-raft-read100
  total ops: 185520
  ops/sec:   12365.9
  p50 (us):  245
  p95 (us):  2611
  p99 (us):  4068

after (amortization enabled):
command: nkv-bench --bench --host 127.0.0.1 --port <leader> --duration 15 --clients 8 --ratio 100 --label read-confirm-raft-read100
  total ops: 4531346
  ops/sec:   151044
  p50 (us):  44
  p95 (us):  104
  p99 (us):  140

delta: 12.2x throughput, p50 5.6x lower, p95 25.1x lower, p99 29.1x
lower. A GET on the Raft leader now costs roughly what a single-node
GET costs (single-node read-only baseline: 198,751 ops/sec, p50=27us —
see optimization-baseline-2026-08-24.txt) instead of paying a full network round
trip to both followers on every single call.

--- 80/20 mix (ratio 80) — the standard workload ---

before (amortization disabled):
command: nkv-bench --bench --host 127.0.0.1 --port <leader> --duration 30 --clients 8 --ratio 80 --label before-read-confirm-raft-8020
  total ops: 167108
  ops/sec:   5570.08
  p50 (us):  1297
  p95 (us):  2492
  p99 (us):  3160

after (amortization enabled):
command: nkv-bench --bench --host 127.0.0.1 --port <leader> --duration 30 --clients 8 --ratio 80 --label read-confirm-raft-8020
  total ops: 256336
  ops/sec:   8543.83
  p50 (us):  63
  p95 (us):  3716
  p99 (us):  5046

delta: 1.53x throughput, p50 20.6x lower. p95/p99 actually got *worse*
(2492us -> 3716us, 3160us -> 5046us) — not a bug, a shift in what's
setting the tail. Before, both GET and SET could land in the slow tail
(a GET's own quorum round trip contending with concurrent SET
replication traffic). After, GETs are near-instant and essentially never
show up in the tail at all, so the p95/p99 landmarks now fall
exclusively on the 20% of ops that are SETs — replication traffic
contending only with itself, at a slightly higher latency floor than
before once GETs stopped sharing (and therefore no longer smoothing
out) that contention. Net effect at the workload level: throughput up
1.53x, median latency down 20.6x, worst-case latency for the write
share essentially unchanged — a clear win for the mix as a whole.

--- correctness note ---

Amortization does not weaken linearizability: skipping the round trip
only happens when a majority has acked *this leader, this term* within
the last heartbeat interval (75ms) — see docs/performance-notes.md for
why that's exactly the guarantee a fresh round trip would have provided
anyway. RaftLinearizabilityTest.ThreeClientsSingleKeyFiveHundredOps and
every RaftFailureScenariosTest/RaftLinearizableReadTest case pass
unchanged with amortization enabled.
